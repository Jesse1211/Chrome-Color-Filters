# Colour → Reward/Valuation: Anatomical and Functional Evidence

**Bottom line for the report writer: the claim "human colour vision is not for seeing clearly, it is for VALUATION" must be DROPPED in its current form.** Two of its three components are either unsupported or affirmatively false. A substantially weaker claim is defensible and is given at the end of this document.

## Q1. The pathway — does colour information reach reward/valuation circuitry?

### Takeaway
**DOES NOT SUPPORT (as a specific colour→reward pathway).** There is no evidence of a dedicated or privileged anatomical route from the L−M chromatic channel to OFC, amygdala, or ventral striatum. Chromatic signals reach valuation structures only the way *every* visual attribute does — via the ventral stream (V1→V2→V4→IT→OFC/amygdala) — and the valuation structures themselves are largely **modality- and feature-agnostic value coders**, not colour processors. The "fast subcortical route" alternative is contested and, critically, is not a colour route at all.

### Cited Findings
- The chromatic pathway is traced only as far as the ventral stream: parvocellular LGN layers → "the ventral pathway goes through V2 and V4 to areas of the inferior temporal lobe" — [Evolution of the circuitry for conscious color vision in primates, PMC5306459](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5306459/). No projection to reward structures is described.
- Conway's IT work concludes colour in IT serves **object/category and material perception**: colour-biased regions sit ventral to face patches, yoked to them, at four anatomically predicted locations, and "color could contribute to animate/inanimate, and likely other, object-category assignments" — [Lafer-Sousa & Conway, Nat Neurosci 2013](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn.3555); [The Organization and Operation of Inferior Temporal Cortex](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326701821_The_Organization_and_Operation_of_Inferior_Temporal_Cortex). This is **representation (a)**, not **integration into value (b)**.
- OFC value neurons encode value **abstractly, not as modulated sensory activity**: "Neurons in the OFC encode economic value per se, not as a modulation of sensory processes," and encode value "independently of visuospatial factors and motor responses" — [Padoa-Schioppa & Assad, Nature 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16633341/); [Padoa-Schioppa 2007](https://sites.wustl.edu/padoaschioppalab/files/2021/05/Padoa-Schioppa-2007.pdf). The visual cue is merely the *identifier* of a juice reward; its colour is incidental.
- Primate amygdala neurons encode positive and negative **value** of visual stimuli, reward probability and magnitude, and valence/intensity — [Paton et al., Nature 2006](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature04490); [Neuronal Encoding of Emotional Valence and Intensity in the Monkey Amygdala, J Neurosci 2022](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/42/40/7615). In these studies the coloured/fractal images are **arbitrary learned cues**; no chromatic tuning is reported or claimed.
- The "low road" to amygdala is contested and argued not to exist in primates: Pessoa & Adolphs state "there is no evidence for a functional subcortical route for visual processing in primates," that visual processing of emotional stimuli "occurs no faster than visual processing in the cortex in general," and that the superior colliculus projects mainly to inferior pulvinar whose output "is directed to the cortex and not the amygdala" — [Pessoa & Adolphs, Nat Rev Neurosci 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn2920). Counter-evidence for a pulvinar→amygdala tract exists — [eLife 2018](https://elifesciences.org/articles/40766) — so the debate is live.
- Relevant physiological constraint: the superior colliculus / magnocellular-dominated fast routes are the ones usually proposed as "fast," and the L−M chromatic signal is carried by the *slow, sustained* parvocellular system, which is "minimally sensitive to luminance modulation and maximally sensitive to chromatic modulation at **low** frequencies" — [Parvocellular pathway overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/parvocellular-cell).

### Inferences
- The report's implied architecture (colour has a special line to the reward system) is **backwards relative to the physiology**: the hypothesised fast subcortical route is the one pathway that would be *least* able to carry L−M chromatic information, because that information lives in the slow parvocellular/midget system.
- The distinction the task set out — (a) colour represented in ventral stream vs (b) colour integrated into value computation — resolves cleanly to **(a) only**. Every "colour in a value area" result I found uses colour as an arbitrary discriminative cue, which demonstrates that *learned associations* reach value circuits, not that *chromaticity* does.

### Gaps
- I found **no single-unit study recording OFC, amygdala, or ventral striatum with systematically varied chromatic stimuli** (e.g. isoluminant hue sweeps, cone-isolating stimuli). Searches for OFC chromatic selectivity returned only V1/V2/V4/IT work. I cannot rule out that such a study exists in a form my queries missed, but its absence from targeted searching is itself informative: this is not an established finding.
- Could not verify any human fMRI study contrasting chromatic vs isoluminant stimuli with reward-region ROIs.

## Q2. Is the "valuation" framing defensible at all?

### Takeaway
**PARTIALLY SUPPORTS — but not in the brain-mechanism sense the report needs.** No serious vision scientist describes colour vision as a valuation system. The dominant scholarly framings are **object/material/scene segmentation** and **social signalling**. However, there is a large, solid applied literature showing food colour drives *expected* palatability and flavour — which supports a learned, cognitive expectancy account, not an innate valuation channel.

### Cited Findings
- Standard scholarly framings are informational, not evaluative: colour vision is "important for segmenting scenes and recognizing objects and surface materials" — [Color Vision overview, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/color-vision).
- Conway emphasises **social communication** alongside foraging: colour contributes to face encoding, and skin-tone changes signal emotional state, health and reproductive status — [Paradoxical impact of memory on color appearance of faces, Nat Commun 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-10073-8); [Conway interview, NIH IRP](https://irp.nih.gov/podcast/2021/09/dr-bevil-conway-coloring-inside-the-minds).
- Food colour is "the most important product-intrinsic sensory cue with reference to taste expectations, both for food and drink," and "color creates a psychological expectation for a certain flavor that is often impossible to dislodge" — [Spence, *On the psychological impact of food colour*, Flavour 2015](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s13411-015-0031-3.pdf).
- Colour "systematically affects taste expectations, perceived flavour intensity, and consumption intentions," with colour–ripeness associations a key driver — [Spence & Levitan, Exploring Colour-Taste Links, J Perceptual Imaging 2022](https://library.imaging.org/admin/apis/public/api/ist/website/downloadArticle/jpi/5/0/jpi0149).
- A notable mechanistic dissociation from the primate retina literature: colour circuitry for *action* evolved separately from and much earlier than circuitry for *conscious colour perception* — "humans have completely separate circuitry... dedicated to conscious color 'vision-for-perception' and this circuitry evolved independently and much later than the color vision circuitry that evolved to directly mediate action" — [PMC5306459](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5306459/).

### Inferences
- The last finding is the **most useful real result for the report**, and it is more interesting than the valuation claim: it supports "colour feeds systems that drive action, partly independently of colour-as-experienced." That is a defensible and non-obvious statement. It is still not "valuation."
- Spence's literature is about **learned colour→flavour expectancies in food-specific contexts**. Generalising it to "colour vision is a valuation system" over-extends it by several orders of scope — a screen's blue notification badge is not a ripening fruit.

### Gaps
- Found no review article by Mollon, Gegenfurtner, Shepard or Lotto that frames colour vision in valuation or foraging-decision terms. I could not access a Mollon review full-text to quote a positive statement of what colour vision *is* for; the Sumner & Mollon chapter (Q3) is about selection pressure, not function-in-the-adult.

## Q3. The evolutionary argument — how strong?

### Takeaway
**DOES NOT SUPPORT a settled foraging/valuation function.** This is the sharpest negative finding in the whole investigation, and it comes with an explicit, quotable statement from the field's own authority that the evidence the report would rely on does not exist.

### Cited Findings
- **The decisive quote.** Sumner & Mollon: "The spectroradiometric evidence is consistent with both the folivory and frugivory hypotheses, and tests of intraspecific differences in foraging success between dichromatic and trichromatic individuals **are yet to be reported**. Therefore, **there is at present no primary evidence that distinguishes between the theories**." — [Sumner & Mollon, *Did primate trichromacy evolve for frugivory or folivory?*, Ch. 3](https://vision.psychol.cam.ac.uk/jdmollon/papers/SumnerMollonFrugivory.pdf) (verified by direct text extraction of the PDF).
- The same source lists live possibilities that undercut a simple adaptive story, including that fruit "can more easily be spotted by their lightness or found using other pre-ingestive cues," and that "there may be advantages in being dichromatic, or in having a polymorphic group with dichromacy and different types of trichromacy all present" — [ibid.](https://vision.psychol.cam.ac.uk/jdmollon/papers/SumnerMollonFrugivory.pdf).
- Their own tentative conclusion favours **leaves, not fruit**, for at least some lineages: "leaf-finding was probably important (as well as fruit-finding) in the spread of uniform trichromacy in *Alouatta*, and may now be the most important factor maintaining trichromacy in highly folivorous catarrhine species" — [ibid.](https://vision.psychol.cam.ac.uk/jdmollon/papers/SumnerMollonFrugivory.pdf).
- Independent support for folivory: howler monkey foraging ecology "suggests convergent evolution of routine trichromacy as an adaptation for folivory," with the search for young reddish leaves possibly driving independent evolution on separate continents — [PMC5330884](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5330884/).
- The social-signalling alternative: because skin spectral variation cannot be seen on a furred face, trichromacy may have been "originally selected for the perception of skin colour signalling" — [Changizi et al., *Bare skin, blood and the evolution of primate colour vision*](https://www.changizi.com/uploads/8/3/4/4/83445868/colorface.pdf).
- Ordering evidence against the communication-first account: trichromacy appears to have "been present before it functioned in communication" — [review of the evolution of animal colour vision and visual communication signals](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51402853_A_review_of_the_evolution_of_animal_colour_vision_and_visual_communication_signals).

### Inferences
- The task's own caution was exactly right: "the L−M channel has high SNR for detecting fruit" establishes **detectability, not function**, and Sumner & Mollon confirm that the behavioural test which would convert detectability into function (dichromat vs trichromat foraging success within a species) **had not been reported**. Any report asserting that trichromacy was selected *for* valuation is asserting something its best source says is undetermined.
- Three mutually competing hypotheses (frugivory, folivory, socio-sexual signalling) remain in play. A report that picks one and builds its central metaphor on it is picking a side in an open dispute without saying so.

### Gaps
- Sumner & Mollon is a book chapter (c. 2000s); I could not confirm from a *current* review whether the intraspecific foraging-success test has since been reported. **Flagged: the "yet to be reported" status needs a recency check before publication.** This is the single most important thing to re-verify, because if such a study now exists it would change the strength (in either direction).

## Q4. Does colour drive approach behaviour?

### Takeaway
**PARTIALLY SUPPORTS, at the behavioural level only.** Colour cues can acquire incentive salience through ordinary Pavlovian learning, and removing colour measurably reduces attentional pull and phone use. But none of this is colour-specific valuation machinery — it is general associative learning and general salience, and the one real-world grayscale effect is modest.

### Cited Findings
- Colour can acquire incentive salience by learning: "pairings of a distractor colour with high reward lead that signal to acquire incentive salience through Pavlovian conditioning, becoming desirable in its own right" — [Attentional capture by Pavlovian reward-signalling distractors, PLOS ONE 2019](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0226284); capture persists after rewards are removed (ibid.).
- Crucially, **the form of the cue matters and colour is not privileged**: a lever-CS elicited sign-tracking in some rats while a tone-CS elicited only goal-tracking in all rats — incentive salience depends on CS modality/form, with no evidence of a special chromatic channel — [The Form of a Conditioned Stimulus..., PLOS ONE 2014](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0098163).
- Colour is necessary for the food attentional bias: "attentional bias for high-calorie food was found with coloured images, [but] no significant differences were obtained with greyscale images" — [Hungry for colours?, Psychological Research 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10339-020-00990-8) (title/abstract level; full text was paywalled).
- **Verified grayscale intervention numbers** (use these, not the fabricated ones): screen time reduced by **approximately 20 minutes per day** during the intervention week; all three dimensions of online vigilance (salience, reactibility, monitoring) significantly reduced; **perceived sleep quality was NOT affected**; productivity not improved. Number of unlocks stayed consistent while usage fell — suggesting grayscale reduces appeal *after* unlocking rather than preventing checking — [Dekker & Baumgartner, *Is life brighter when your phone is not?*, Mobile Media & Communication 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20501579231212062).
- Supporting (weaker, smaller) studies: [True colors: Grayscale setting reduces screen time in college students](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/340421051_True_colors_Grayscale_setting_reduces_screen_time_in_college_students); [Suffering from problematic smartphone use? Why not use grayscale setting as an intervention!](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/370584725_Suffering_from_problematic_smartphone_use_Why_not_use_grayscale_setting_as_an_intervention_-_An_experimental_study).

### Inferences
- The behavioural chain that *is* supported: colour increases **salience** → salience sustains engagement → removing colour reduces engagement. This is sufficient to justify the product (a grayscale filter) without any valuation claim at all. **The report does not need the valuation thesis to justify its own existence.**
- The unlocks-unchanged/usage-down pattern is genuinely informative and argues for a *salience/engagement* mechanism rather than a *valuation/wanting* mechanism — if colour were setting the anticipated value of the phone, you would expect fewer reaches for it, not equal reaches with shorter sessions.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found that contrasts colour vs greyscale stimuli with **reward-system neuroimaging**. The only candidate is the [Research Square preprint](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-7832668/v1), which is **not peer reviewed and contains no neuroimaging**. Treat as [UNSUPPORTED].
- No peer-reviewed willingness-to-pay experiment manipulating saturation of food images was verified. Marketing claims to this effect surfaced only in unsourced summary text.

## Q5. The two side-claims

### Takeaway
Side-claim (a) "colour vision is not for seeing clearly" is **a category error AND partly false as stated** — but the correct version is subtler than the task's proposed fix. Side-claim (b) is **CONFIRMED verbatim**.

### Cited Findings — (a) acuity
- The parvocellular/midget system **is** the acuity pathway: "The midget ganglion cells project to the parvocellular (PC) layer in the LGN and mediate the 'red–green' chromatic opponency signal **and spatial acuity**"; midget RGCs are described as doing "double-duty" mediating both high-acuity and colour vision — [How We See Black and White: The Role of Midget Ganglion Cells, PMC9294633](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9294633/); [Transmission of colour and acuity signals by parvocellular cells in marmoset monkeys](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51045453_Transmission_of_colour_and_acuity_signals_by_parvocellular_cells_in_marmoset_monkeys).
- The **chromatic difference signal is low-resolution**: humans resolve achromatic gratings at 30–60 cycles/degree but isoluminant red–green gratings at under ~10–12 cycles/degree — [The spatio-chromatic sensitivity of the human visual system](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/12218380_The_spatio-chromatic_sensitivity_of_the_human_visual_system); chromatic acuity also declines more steeply with eccentricity — [Anderson et al., J Physiol 1991](https://physoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1113/jphysiol.1991.sp018781).
- **Important complication the task did not anticipate:** at least one strand of the literature *rejects* the double-duty framing, arguing L/M midget cells are primarily achromatic edge detectors and that "the much larger population of L/M opponent achromatic edge detectors can be completely agnostic as to whether an edge is produced by a chromatic or achromatic change," with hue carried by a sparse, low-resolution subset — "'blue' and 'yellow' are sampled at much lower spatial resolution but nonetheless the much more sparse cortical representations of hue are able to crudely—more like a watercolor—fill in the colors" — [PMC5306459](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5306459/).

### Cited Findings — (b) attentional guidance
- **Verified by direct PDF text extraction.** Wolfe & Horowitz (2017), Box 1: "**Undoubted guiding attributes** • Colour • Motion • Orientation • Size (including length, spatial frequency, and apparent size)". "**Probable guiding attributes** • Luminance onset (flicker) but see ref. 121 • Luminance polarity • Vernier offset • Stereoscopic depth and tilt • Pictorial depth cues • Shape • Line termination • Closure • Curvature • Topological status."
- The stated criterion: "Attributes like colour are deemed to be 'undoubted' because multiple experiments from multiple labs attest to their ability to guide attention. 'Probable' feature dimensions may be merely probable because we are not sure how to define the feature." — [Wolfe & Horowitz, *Five factors that guide attention in visual search*, Nature Human Behaviour 1:0058, 2017](https://search.bwh.harvard.edu/new/pubs/FiveFactors_Wolfe-Horowitz_2017.pdf)
- Also notable from the same Box 1, and useful against over-claiming: "**Probably not guiding attributes** • ... • **Colour change** • ... • **Threat** • Semantic category..." and in the text, "Nor does threat (defined by association with electric shock) seem to guide search." — [ibid.](https://search.bwh.harvard.edu/new/pubs/FiveFactors_Wolfe-Horowitz_2017.pdf)

### Inferences
- The precise, defensible formulation: **the L+M luminance signal carries spatial acuity; the L−M chromatic difference signal is low-resolution — yet both are carried by the same midget/parvocellular cells.** So "colour vision isn't for seeing clearly" is wrong at the level of *cells and pathway* (the colour pathway IS the acuity pathway) and right only at the level of the *chromatic difference signal*. The report must name which level it means or it is simply incorrect.
- Side-claim (b) survives, and with a bonus: Wolfe & Horowitz rank colour above luminance contrast **and** classify "threat" and "colour change" as non-guiding. The report should not pair the colour-salience point with any claim that threat or affective content guides search — the same source denies it.

### Gaps
- Wolfe & Horowitz is about *attentional guidance in visual search*, not motivation or reward. It cannot support a valuation claim; it supports only salience. Do not let it be cited for more.

### Strongest version the evidence will actually bear
> Colour is one of a small set of visual attributes that reliably and automatically guides attention — Wolfe and Horowitz class it among the four "undoubted" guiding attributes, ahead of luminance contrast, which they rank only "probable." Colour also readily acquires learned incentive value: a hue paired with reward captures attention even after the reward stops. And in food contexts, colour is the dominant cue for *expected* palatability. Stripping colour from a display therefore removes a first-tier salience signal and the learned colour–reward associations built on top of it — which is why a week of grayscale cut smartphone use by roughly twenty minutes a day while leaving the number of unlocks unchanged: the phone became less compelling once opened, rather than less reachable.

Every clause above is individually sourced in this document. Note it makes **no** anatomical claim and **no** evolutionary claim.

### The weakest link a hostile reviewer attacks first
**The evolutionary premise, and they will attack it with Mollon.** The report leans on frugivory as if settled; Sumner & Mollon state in print that "there is at present no primary evidence that distinguishes between the theories" and themselves lean toward *folivory*. A reviewer only needs that one sentence to remove the report's foundation. The second attack is on "valuation" itself: asked to name the pathway, the report has none — and the most-cited OFC result says value neurons encode value "per se, not as a modulation of sensory processes," which is close to the opposite of what the report needs.

### Recommended replacement term
Use **"salience"** as the primary term, with **"learned affective tagging"** for the conditioning component.

Justification: "salience" is the term the evidence actually licenses — it is Wolfe & Horowitz's own domain, it is what the grayscale studies measure (online vigilance: *salience*, reactibility, monitoring), and it requires no claim about reward anatomy. "Valuation" is a technical term in neuroeconomics denoting computation of a common-currency decision variable in OFC/vmPFC/striatum; using it for colour asserts something specific, checkable, and currently false. "Foraging-relevant signalling" is defensible for the *evolutionary* discussion but must carry the frugivory/folivory/social-signalling dispute with it.

**Suggested rewrite of the offending sentence:** "Colour is not primarily a channel for spatial detail — that work is done by the luminance signal. Colour is a salience channel: it is among the few features that automatically steer attention, and it readily absorbs learned associations with reward." This keeps the rhetorical shape, drops the false and unsupported parts, and is fully sourced.
